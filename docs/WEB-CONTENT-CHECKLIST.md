# Web content and search review checklist

Use this checklist alongside the project's private `web-taste.md`. It is a review aid, not an automatic SEO score, ranking guarantee or claim of verified facts. Apply only relevant criteria. Never invent reviews, credentials, locations, service facts, experience, prices, authorship or structured-data claims.

For every item record: **not assessed / supported / needs work / not applicable**, the page/section, evidence, proposed change and human decision. Mark missing evidence explicitly. Layout experiments do not approve new taste rules.

1. **Primary H1 and page intent.** Does one clear primary heading identify the page's principal topic or service without keyword stuffing?
2. **Local relevance.** Where useful, are real location, service area, directions and contact details consistent and supported?
3. **Trust.** Are identity, contact methods, policies and meaningful credibility signals visible and accurate?
4. **Experience and expertise evidence.** Does relevant first-hand work, qualified authorship or sourced expertise support the claims? Treat E-E-A-T as a review concept, not a numeric score.
5. **Problem and solution.** Does the page explain the visitor's need and how the offering addresses it?
6. **Useful FAQ.** Are genuine visitor questions answered directly without fabricated questions or unnecessary repetition?
7. **Internal links.** Are related services, evidence and next steps connected with meaningful links?
8. **Substantive service cards.** Do service summaries provide specific, evidenced information instead of empty labels?
9. **Topical coverage.** Does the page cover the topic sufficiently for its actual audience and purpose?
10. **Social proof.** Are real reviews, examples or outcomes accurately attributed and presented with appropriate context?
11. **Comparisons.** Where helpful, are options or tradeoffs explained fairly, with sources for factual claims?
12. **Clear CTA.** Is the primary next step understandable, appropriate and functional?
13. **Machine-readable clarity.** Is the important information available as accessible text with clear entities and relationships? Do not promise inclusion or visibility in AI search.
14. **Structured data opportunities.** Is a relevant supported schema type appropriate, consistent with visible content and eligible under the target platform's current rules? Validate before adding it; schema is not a visibility guarantee.
15. **Supporting sections.** Do examples, process details, team information, policies or other supporting blocks resolve real visitor questions without adding filler?
